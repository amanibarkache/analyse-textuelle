Artificial intelligence is reshaping the global economy at a pace never seen before in human history. According to a 2023 Goldman Sachs report, AI-powered automation could replace the equivalent of 300 million full-time jobs worldwide — roughly 18% of the global workforce. Yet the same technology is expected to create entirely new categories of work : between 2023 and 2030, demand for AI specialists, data scientists and machine learning engineers is projected to grow by 40%. The net economic impact remains deeply uncertain — estimates range from a 7% boost to global GDP to a 15% contraction in labour income for the bottom 50% of earners.

The digital revolution has concentrated extraordinary wealth in the hands of a remarkably small number of technology companies. In 2023, the five largest American tech firms — Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta — collectively held more cash reserves than the GDP of Switzerland. Apple alone generated 394 billion dollars in revenue in 2022, more than the entire economic output of Denmark. This concentration of economic power raises fundamental questions about market competition, tax avoidance — tech giants shift an estimated 200 billion dollars annually through low-tax jurisdictions — and the long-term sustainability of democratic governance in the digital age.

Climate technology start-ups attracted record levels of investment in 2022, with venture capital firms pouring 70 billion dollars into clean energy, carbon capture and sustainable agriculture. Battery technology — the critical bottleneck for renewable energy storage — has seen its cost fall from 1,200 dollars per kilowatt-hour in 2010 to just 132 dollars in 2022, an 89% reduction in 12 years. Electric vehicle sales surpassed 10 million units globally in 2022, representing 14% of all new cars sold — up from less than 1% in 2015. Tesla, founded in 2003 with 30 employees and zero revenue, reached a market capitalisation of 1,200 billion dollars in 2021, briefly making it the 5th most valuable company on Earth.

Social media platforms have fundamentally altered the way information spreads, political movements organise and mental health evolves across societies. A landmark study tracking 500,000 teenagers across 72 countries found that each additional hour of daily social media use increased the risk of depression and anxiety by 27%. Misinformation spreads 6 times faster on Twitter than accurate news, according to a 2018 MIT study analysing 126,000 stories shared by 3 million users between 2006 and 2017. By 2023, TikTok had surpassed 1 billion active monthly users, with its algorithm reportedly capable of identifying a user's political leanings, sexual orientation and mental health status within 3 hours of first use.

Cybersecurity has become one of the most critical — and most underfunded — challenges facing governments, hospitals and financial institutions worldwide. In 2021, a ransomware attack on Colonial Pipeline, which supplies 45% of the fuel consumed on America's East Coast, shut down operations for 6 days and triggered fuel shortages across 17 states. Global cybercrime costs are estimated to reach 10.5 trillion dollars annually by 2025, making it — if it were a country — the world's third largest economy after the United States and China. Healthcare systems are particularly vulnerable : hospital cyberattacks increased by 300% between 2019 and 2022, with patient data from more than 40 million Americans compromised in breaches reported in 2021 alone.

The race for quantum computing supremacy represents the next frontier in the technological competition between great powers. In 2019, Google claimed to have achieved "quantum supremacy" — performing in 200 seconds a calculation that would take the world's fastest classical supercomputer 10,000 years. China invested more than 15 billion dollars in quantum research between 2016 and 2023, compared to approximately 3.7 billion dollars by the United States government. A functional quantum computer powerful enough to break current encryption standards could render all existing digital security infrastructure — banking systems, military communications, medical records — instantly obsolete.
